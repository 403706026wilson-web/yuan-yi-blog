A Technique Older Than History

Lost wax casting — also called investment casting — is one of humanity's oldest metalworking techniques. The earliest known examples date back over 6,000 years to the Indus Valley civilization. Today, it's still the preferred method for creating fine jewelry with intricate detail.

How It Works

The process is elegant in its simplicity:

  • Step 1: A wax model is created — either hand-carved by a master craftsman or 3D-printed from a digital design. This wax model is an exact replica of the final piece.
  • Step 2: The wax model is attached to a "tree" (a central wax stem) and encased in a heat-resistant plaster called investment.
  • Step 3: The plaster mold is heated in a kiln. The wax melts and runs out — "lost" — leaving a perfect negative cavity of the jewelry piece.
  • Step 4: Molten metal (silver, gold, or platinum) is poured into the cavity. It fills every detail — every engraving, every setting, every curve.
  • Step 5: The plaster is broken away, revealing the raw metal casting. Sprue attachments are cut, and the piece is hand-finished.
🔍 Behind the Scenes: At YUAN YI, each wax model is inspected under 10x magnification before casting. Any imperfection in the wax will appear in the final metal — so we catch it early.

Why Lost Wax?

Lost wax casting captures details that machine cutting cannot — undercuts, fine filigree, complex geometries. It's why your YUAN YI ring has the same crisp detail on the inside as the outside. It's also why each piece has a subtle uniqueness — the hallmark of a handcrafted object.
